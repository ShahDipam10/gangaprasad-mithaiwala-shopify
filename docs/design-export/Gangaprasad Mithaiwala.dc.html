Since 1919, one family, one recipe book.

Anand is known across India as the country's dairy capital — home to the cooperative movement that built Amul. It's also where, in 1919, Gangaprasad opened a single mithai counter, using milk collected from the same cooperative dairies that morning.

More than a century later, the shop is still run by his descendants, three generations on. The recipes haven't changed, and neither has the habit: small batches, made fresh every day, with no preservatives — because nothing here is meant to sit on a shelf for long.
